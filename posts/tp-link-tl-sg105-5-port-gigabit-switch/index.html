The problem it solves

Most home routers have four Ethernet ports on the back. A homelab fills them fast: a server or mini PC, a NAS, a Raspberry Pi, a desktop. A small unmanaged switch fixes that with one cable: plug one of its ports into the router and the other four become extra wired ports on the same network.

What it is

  • 5 gigabit Ethernet ports (10/100/1000 Mbps, auto-negotiation on every port)
  • Metal case, no fan, so it is silent
  • Unmanaged and plug-and-play: no app, no web page, nothing to configure
  • Energy-efficient design, which matters for a box that runs 24/7 on a shelf
  • Sits on a desk or mounts on a wall
  • Works behind any router, including an OPNsense or pfSense box

Where it fits in a homelab

  • Under the desk: one cable from the router, four wired devices next to you.
  • Next to the server shelf: the mini PC running Proxmox, the NAS and the Pi all get wired gigabit links instead of Wi-Fi.
  • Behind the TV: a console, a streaming box and a smart TV on one wall jack.

What it does not do

It is unmanaged, so there are no VLANs, no port settings and no traffic graphs. If you want to split IoT devices onto their own VLAN, you need a managed or "easy smart" switch instead (TP-Link sells those under other model numbers). If you only need more ports, unmanaged is simpler and there is nothing to misconfigure. There is also an 8-port version if five is not enough.
