What the episode covers, with the articles

Backups (02:39). The green check on a Proxmox backup job proves the job wrote some bytes somewhere, nothing more. The hosts walk through the 3-2-1 plan, keep-daily=7 and why it is not seven days of history, the backup job that silently skips every guest you created after you made it, Proxmox Backup Server on its own hardware, the four restore drills, the notification system and the dead-man's switch for jobs that stop running.

  • Proxmox backups that actually work: 3-2-1, step by step
  • keep-daily=7 doesn't mean 7 days of backups
  • Your Proxmox backup job is probably skipping your new VMs
  • A backup job that never runs never fails

Storage (14:36). The NFS share that drops at boot and lets a backup job fill the hypervisor's root disk, and the two-word fix (is_mountpoint). Why RAID and ZFS mirrors are one copy on resilient disks, why snapshots die with the pool, and the sanoid + syncoid pull-replication setup with a monthly checksum restore.

  • When your NFS share drops, Proxmox backups fill your root disk
  • RAID is not a backup: the 3-2-1 rule, honestly
  • Snapshots on a schedule, replicas on another box: sanoid and syncoid pull replication for ZFS

Networking (21:09). What the /24 after an address actually says, why one flat network for the whole house is the trap, the five-zone plan from Lock Down Your Home Network, and why a two-node Proxmox cluster freezes the moment one node goes down (one vote out of two is not more than half). The QDevice fix, the Wi-Fi trap and the pvecm expected 1 emergency lever.

  • What a /24 subnet actually means
  • Why your 2-node Proxmox cluster freezes

Self-hosting (27:04). Immich, Nextcloud or Syncthing, and Vaultwarden on one small box, and the six traps around them: one disk for everything, the Immich database on a network share, the hand-built Nextcloud, open signups and plain HTTP on Vaultwarden, forwarding ports to everything, and the backup that isn't one.

  • Self-host your photos, files and passwords the safe way

Home Assistant (32:43). The motion light that turns off while you are still in the hallway (mode: single is the culprit; mode: restart plus a wait_for_trigger with a for: is the fix), and the Zigbee fix that beats any setting: the coordinator on a USB 2.0 extension cable, away from the case and the access point.

  • New book: Automations That Just Work (Home Assistant without the surprises)

The turtle tank (37:36). The Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3 water monitor from Smart Turtle Tank: the one wire that can fry the board (the turbidity sensor's 4.5 V output into a 3.3 V pin, and the voltage divider that fixes it), the DS18B20 readings of 85 °C and -127 °C that are not temperatures at all, and the three numbers a tank has to get right before any electronics go near it.

  • The one wire that can fry your ESP32 tank monitor
  • DS18B20 reads 85 °C or -127 °C? Here's why
  • 3 numbers your turtle tank has to get right
