The problem it solves

Every homelab has one box that hangs: the cheap mini PC, the old router, the Raspberry Pi with the flaky SD card. When it locks up, the fix is pulling the power cord. A Wi-Fi smart plug turns that into a tap in an app, from the couch or from anywhere.

What it is

  • A Wi-Fi plug on 2.4 GHz, no hub needed
  • On and off from the Kasa app, at home or away
  • Schedules, timers and an away mode
  • Works with Alexa and Google Home
  • Home Assistant has an integration for Kasa devices
  • Ultra-mini size, so two fit in one outlet
  • Sold as a 2-pack

Where it fits

  • Remote hard reboot: put the box that freezes behind one and power-cycle it when it stops answering.
  • Schedules: desk lights, a monitor, or a test box that only needs to run in the evening.
  • Home Assistant: automate it with everything else, for example cut power to a hung device after a ping check fails (do this with care; see below).

What it does not do

A smart plug cuts power hard; it does not shut a machine down cleanly. Do not use one to switch off a NAS or a server in the middle of writes, and never put it in front of the device that runs your network or your Home Assistant, or you lose the way to turn it back on. Use it for things that are safe to yank, and keep a proper shutdown for anything with data on it. Plugs on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi also depend on your Wi-Fi being up.
