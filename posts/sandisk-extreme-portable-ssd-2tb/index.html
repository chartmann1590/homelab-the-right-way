The problem it solves

A lot of desks still back up to a spinning USB hard drive: slow, heavy, and one bad drop away from losing everything on it. A portable SSD has no moving parts, so it is faster and much harder to break in a bag.

What it is

  • Solid-state, no moving parts
  • USB-C, USB 3.2 Gen 2
  • Read speeds up to 1050 MB/s (SanDisk's figure)
  • Up to 3-meter drop protection and IP65 water and dust resistance
  • 256-bit AES hardware encryption with password protection
  • Pocket-size, with a carabiner loop to clip it to a bag
  • Also sold in 1TB and 4TB

Where it fits

  • The "1" in 3-2-1: a copy of your photos, documents or VM backups that lives somewhere other than the machine it came from.
  • Moving big files: a full laptop image or a stack of VM exports in minutes instead of an evening.
  • Travel: a backup copy that rides along without worrying about bumps.

What it does not do

One drive is not a backup strategy on its own. If it is your only copy, it is a single point of failure like any other disk. Keep at least one more copy somewhere else, and test a restore now and then. The book Never Lose Your Data walks through the whole 3-2-1 setup.
